A history of discovery

Milestones across the Solar System

A selection of turning points represented in this archive, from the first successful lunar soft landings to powered flight on Mars.

1959

Luna 2 reaches the Moon

The first human-made object to reach the lunar surface. This archive focuses on later NASA missions and artifacts.

1966–1967

Surveyor demonstrates lunar soft landing

NASA’s Surveyor program returned close-up images and surface measurements that helped prepare for Apollo.

1969–1972

Apollo leaves a scientific footprint

Apollo crews deploy experiments, use lunar rovers and leave descent stages and equipment at landing sites.

1976

Viking lands on Mars

Viking 1 and Viking 2 become the first U.S. missions to operate successfully on the Martian surface.

1997

Sojourner explores Mars

The small rover demonstrates mobile robotic exploration on another planet.

2004

Spirit and Opportunity begin exploring

The twin rovers find extensive evidence of past water activity on Mars.

2011–2015

Dawn explores Vesta and Ceres

One spacecraft visits two major bodies in the asteroid belt, using ion propulsion.

2021–2024

Ingenuity proves powered flight on Mars

The technology demonstrator completes 72 flights before rotor damage ends its flight operations.

Why these missions matter

Discoveries that outlast a mission

The archive is about more than where spacecraft ended up. It is about what they taught us.

Evidence of ancient water

Spirit and Opportunity found geological evidence that liquid water once altered rocks on Mars. Their findings reshaped the search for habitable environments.

Worlds beyond the familiar

Dawn revealed Vesta and Ceres in unprecedented detail, helping scientists understand the early Solar System and the diversity of small worlds.

Technology that opens new paths

Sojourner demonstrated planetary mobility, while Ingenuity showed that powered, controlled flight is possible in the thin atmosphere of Mars.

Listening to distant worlds

ALSEP instruments continued collecting lunar science after astronauts departed. InSight later used seismic measurements to study the interior of Mars.

About the project

Preserving the echoes

Echoes of Exploration is an independent educational archive by Team Evenstar. It celebrates the engineering, science and curiosity behind humanity’s robotic journeys into space.

Project note

Mission summaries are written for general audiences and linked to primary NASA sources. Some mission records group related hardware or missions for clarity; individual launch, landing and end dates are shown separately where available. NASA images are fetched from NASA’s public image library when the browser can reach its API. If an image cannot load, the mission card remains readable and its NASA source link is still available.
